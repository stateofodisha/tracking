A Position Paper

Southern Odisha Needs
An Airport At Brahmapur

Palasa — a town of 57,000 people across the Andhra border — is getting a full greenfield airport. Brahmapur, Odisha's fourth-largest city, sits on a runway-ready airstrip that keeps getting downgraded instead of built. Here's the case for fixing that.

01 · The Core Argument

Odisha's fourth-largest city still has no real airport.

Brahmapur (Berhampur) is Odisha's oldest trading centre for the entire south — a city of over 355,000 people that, by its own development authority's account, holds commercial influence over eight districts. It has a university, a medical college, an IISER campus, a GI-tagged silk industry, and a working deep-sea port fifteen minutes away. What it doesn't have is a functioning commercial airport.

The land already exists. The Rangeilunda airstrip near Brahmapur has been earmarked for aviation use for years — and instead of becoming the full greenfield airport southern Odisha has asked for, its scope was quietly reduced to a runway upgrade after the original proposal was shelved in 2025. Meanwhile, across the state border, Palasa — a town roughly a sixth of Brahmapur's size — is going through final clearance for a brand-new greenfield airport.

03 · Anchor and Spoke

Brahmapur doesn't compete with Bhubaneswar. It completes it.

Bhubaneswar-Cuttack is, and should remain, Odisha's primary international anchor — the case for consolidating wide-body and long-haul capacity there is straightforward. But a state this large can't run on one airport alone. Brahmapur sits 169 km south of Bhubaneswar and 255 km north of Visakhapatnam — close enough to feed the anchor by road or short domestic hop, but far enough that today, anyone in southern Odisha drives three to four hours each way just to catch a flight.

That's exactly the gap a spoke airport is meant to close: regional and domestic routes into Brahmapur, connecting onward through Bhubaneswar for international travel. Southern Odisha gets real air access. The anchor airport gets a feeder market instead of losing that traffic to Visakhapatnam across the state line.

04 · The Economic Case

The region is industrialising faster than its connectivity.

Gopalpur Port, fifteen minutes from Brahmapur, was acquired by Adani Ports in 2024 and is now the subject of a ₹16,554 crore expansion — taking capacity from 20 to 50 million tonnes a year and creating an estimated 5,000 direct jobs. A new deep-water port at Bahuda, also in Ganjam district, carries a separate ₹21,500 crore investment. Tata Steel is developing a 1,400-acre green hydrogen and green ammonia hub adjacent to Gopalpur Port, with ACME, Avaada, Ocior, and Torrent all setting up production units on the same site.

None of that investment is well served by a three-to-four-hour road trip to the nearest airport. Executives, technical staff, and cargo tied to a fifty-million-tonne port and a green hydrogen SEZ need scheduled air access nearby — not a drive to Bhubaneswar that eats most of a working day each way.

05 · The Tourism Case

Southern Odisha's tourist circuit has no air gateway of its own.

Gopalpur-on-Sea, Taratarini Temple, the southern shores of Chilika at Rambha and Barkul, and the hot springs at Taptapani all sit within an hour of Brahmapur — a genuine coastal-and-hill tourism circuit that today can only be reached by road from Bhubaneswar. Brahmapur is also the natural staging point for tribal and eco-tourism further into Rayagada, Koraput, and the Eastern Ghats, a circuit international visitors currently have almost no direct way to reach.

Puri already carries the state's pilgrimage tourism load. Brahmapur's tourism case is different and complementary — coastal leisure, temple heritage, and gateway access to the southern highlands — and it currently has no air infrastructure supporting any of it.

06 · Connectivity Already In Place

This isn't a connectivity gap. It's an aviation gap.

Unlike a lot of proposed airport sites, Brahmapur doesn't need its surface connectivity built from scratch — most of it already exists or is actively under construction.

07 · Why Palasa Isn't The Answer

Palasa solves a cross-border problem. It doesn't solve Odisha's.

Palasa makes sense on its own terms — Andhra Pradesh is running an aggressive statewide airport-building program, and a small town near the Odisha border is a reasonable node in that network. But it was never designed to serve southern Odisha's population, industry, or tourism circuit. It serves northern Andhra Pradesh, with Odisha traffic as an afterthought at best.

If Brahmapur doesn't get its own airport, southern Odisha's real choice becomes: drive four hours to Bhubaneswar, or drive across a state border to a town a sixth of Brahmapur's size that was never planned around Odisha's needs. Neither is a substitute for building on the land Odisha already has, in the city that already anchors the region.

"It is mainly a trading centre for Southern Odisha having its influence over eight districts."

08 · Not Either/Or

One anchor, and the spokes it actually needs.

This isn't an argument against Bhubaneswar's primacy, or against Palasa existing at all. It's an argument that Odisha's own southern region — its fourth-largest city, an expanding port economy, a real tourism circuit, and eight districts of commercial reach — deserves an airport sized to what's already there, on land that's already earmarked, instead of watching that plan get quietly downgraded while a smaller town across the border gets the real thing.

Brahmapur doesn't need to be Odisha's second Bhubaneswar. It needs to be the airport southern Odisha has been asking for since long before Palasa's runway was ever proposed.

The land is there. The port is expanding. The roads are being built. The airport isn't.

Southern Odisha has the population, the industry, and the connectivity to support a real airport at Brahmapur. What it's had instead is a downgraded runway plan while Palasa's greenfield airport moves toward final clearance.
